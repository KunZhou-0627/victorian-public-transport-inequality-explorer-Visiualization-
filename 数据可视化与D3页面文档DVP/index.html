Victorian public transport access

Some places are connected on paper, but disconnected in daily life.

A station, stop, or route can suggest that an area is connected, but it does not necessarily mean that public transport is usable in everyday life. For residents, the real question is whether services are frequent enough, run late enough, operate reliably on weekends, and provide more than one practical travel option. This project therefore looks beyond the simple presence of infrastructure and focuses on the service conditions that shape day-to-day mobility.

This explorer asks: Where does the network look available, but feel difficult to use?

This visualisation tells the story in stages. It begins with the map, which shows how public transport service gaps are distributed across Victorian LGAs. The ranking chart then identifies the most underserved areas under the selected metric. From there, users can select up to three LGAs on the map or ranking chart to inspect and compare their inequality profiles, including the factors behind each score and how late services continue to run. The story then widens again through the weekday-versus-weekend comparison, the coverage-and-intensity bubble chart, and the conclusion matrix, which summarise whether disadvantage is driven by limited coverage, weak frequency, short service span, poor weekend availability, or lack of mode diversity.
